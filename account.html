Privacy

File hosting

Photos and short clips are sent inside the chat. Anything bigger, and any other kind of file, is uploaded to a free file hosting service and the chat gets a link. Choose which services may be used and in what order. These choices are saved on this device.

Each service uses the time it offers that is closest to yours; if you pick longer than it can do, it uses its longest. Files are never set to be deleted after the first download.

If a service fails, or can't take the file (too big for it), the next switched-on service in the list is tried. A switched-off service is never used.

Rules that apply to all of them
  • Uploads are anonymous but public to anyone who has the link. Don't upload anything private unless the chat is end-to-end encrypted (then the file is scrambled before it leaves your device).
  • These services scan uploads and remove malware, copyrighted material and abuse; illegal content is not allowed.
  • Free services can be slow, rate-limit you or disappear. That's why the list falls back to the next one; when the service you want is busy, wait a moment and retry.
  • Limits and times shown here come from each service's own information and can change. Check their terms on their websites.

Screen sharing: defaults

Starting quality for when you share your screen. These settings only change what you send; what you watch from others is always the best the sender provides (your browser lowers it by itself if needed). When you press Share screen in a call you see these settings again and can change them for that share, or start with them as they are. Higher settings use more bandwidth, CPU and battery, and the people in the call need a connection that can keep up.

Encryption

Chats are end-to-end encrypted by default. Your key lives on this device; the recovery code is the only way to unlock it elsewhere.

Key fingerprint: …

Encrypted chats are locked on this device. Unencrypted chats work as usual. Enter your recovery code whenever you have it and everything unlocks.

Lost your recovery code?

You can reset your encryption keys, but it is a last resort: your old keys on other devices stop working and older encrypted messages may stay unreadable. Try your recovery code or another signed-in device first.
